STANDATA variance 23-BCV-008(REV1) 
Building 
Residential exhaust outlet clearance from air intakes 
Date Issued: October 2024 
Page 1 of 4 
Purpose 
Provides an alternative solution to Sentence 9.32.3.13.(4) for the minimum clearance requirement for domestic 
exhaust outlets that discharge air containing moisture, such as kitchen, bathroom, and clothes dryer exhaust 
outlets, from air intakes. 
Discussion 
The National Building Code of Canada 2020 introduced changes to Article 9.32.3.13. for minimum clearance 
requirements related to exhaust vents, soffits, and air intakes. The National Building Code – 2023 Alberta Edition 
(NBC(AE)) subsequently harmonized with these changes.  
The minimum clearance required between a gas vent or oil fill pipe and a mechanically vented air intake was 
increased from 900 mm to 1 800 mm in Sentence 9.32.3.13.(3).  
Additionally, new Sentences (4) to (6) were introduced to address the minimum clearance required from exhaust 
outlets discharging moisture-laden air to soffits and air intakes. While the national code change rationale for 
Sentences (4) to (6) specifically explained the application of the 1 800 mm minimum clearance requirement to 
soffits, it did not address the application to air intakes as provided in Sentence (4). A required 1 800 mm minimum 
clearance between moist-air exhaust outlets and air intakes for domestic applications was not explained and other 
standards suggest it can be potentially reduced. 
CSA-B149.1, “Natural gas and propane installation code,” (CSA B149.1) provides minimum clearances between 
clothes dryer vent terminations and air intakes: 900 mm for domestic clothes dryers and 1 800 mm for commercial 
clothes dryers. As domestic clothes dryer exhaust air typically contains more moisture than bathroom or kitchen 
exhaust air, this supports the basis for a variance that reduces the 1 800 mm minimum required clearance 
between a moist-air exhaust outlet and air intake specified by Sentence 9.32.3.13.(4) of the NBC(AE). 
As air intakes located above moist-air exhaust outlets are more susceptible to the effects of infiltration (due to 
the rising of warm, moist air), the reduction in the required minimum clearance from 1 800 mm to 900 mm 
provided in this variance is only applicable to where the moist-air exhaust outlet is located horizontally to the air 
intake or above it. This maintains the rationale for the changes to Article 9.32.3.13. in the NBC(AE) while 
providing appropriate reductions in the required minimum clearance for certain dimensions (see Figure A). 
Air intakes specified by Sentence (4) refer to intakes supplying ventilation air to the building and includes those 
connected to HRV’s/ERV’s or the return air plenum of a furnace. Note that in cases where the fresh air intake 
connected to the return air plenum of a furnace is less than 8 inches in diameter, it can be considered as a 
“building opening” rather than an air intake (as the CSA B149.1 standard also allows for).  
Moist-air exhaust outlets specified by Sentence (4) include not only bathroom ventilation and clothes dryer 
exhaust outlets, but kitchen exhaust outlets as well, despite kitchen exhaust outlets not being explicitly mentioned 
in the Sentence itself or in the rationale for the code change.

Code References 
Sentences 9.32.3.13.(3) to (6) 
9.32.3.13. Outdoor Intake and Exhaust Openings 
3) The distance separating air intakes for mechanical ventilation from exhaust outlets that are potential 
sources of contaminants, such as gas vents or oil fill pipes, shall be not less than 1 800 mm. 
 
4) Except as provided in Sentences (5) and (6), exhaust outlets that discharge air containing moisture, 
such as bathroom ventilation and clothes dryer exhaust outlets, shall be located at least 1 800 mm from 
air intakes and vented soffits. 
 
5) Where an exhaust outlet referred to in Sentence (4) is located within a soffit, the soffit shall either be 
unvented, or if vented, the full depth of the soffit shall be blocked for a distance of 1 800 mm on each side 
of the exhaust outlet. 
 
6) Where an exhaust outlet referred to in Sentence (4) is located in a sidewall less than 1 800 mm from a 
soffit, a section of the soffit above the exhaust outlet shall be unvented, or if vented, the full depth of the 
soffit shall be blocked in accordance with the widths stipulated in Table 9.32.3.13.-A, centred over the 
location of the outlet. 
 
 
CSA-B149.1 Clauses 7.5.1 and 7.5.2

Rationale for Clearance Change 
Problem 
Exhaust from clothes dryers and bathroom fans is allowed to discharge from vents installed in the soffits 
and in the side walls below the soffits. This warm, moist air can enter the attic space through any vented 
soffits in the immediate vicinity of the exhaust vent, potentially causing mold, mildew, rot, loss of the 
insulation’s R-value, ice damming, and water infiltration. Approximately five per cent of houses with soffit 
exhaust vents conforming to the current code requirements show signs of mold, mildew, rot, and water 
infiltration.

Installing unvented soffits or blocking a section of vented soffits for a certain width on each side of exhaust 
vents will prevent the infiltration of warm, moist air from the exhaust vents into the attic space, which can 
cause mold, mildew, rot, loss of the insulation’s R-value, ice damming, and water infiltration. 
Air containing moisture rises from the exhaust outlets and infiltrates the attic through the soffits, then 
condensation builds up in the attic over time, resulting in the growth of mold, which causes the 
deterioration of the ceiling below the roof. Most homeowners do not realize that the roof sheathing on their 
house is rotting until they replace the roof or notice mold on an interior finish—mold that could be affecting 
the health of the occupants. 
This proposed change is based on the provisions of CSA B139, “Installation code for Oil-Burning 
equipment” which stipulates that side wall vents need to be a minimum 1.8m from soffits or unvented 
soffits must be installed. 
 
Application 
This variance applies to exhaust outlets that discharge air containing moisture, such as kitchen, bathroom and 
clothes dryer exhaust outlets, serving an individual dwelling unit or a house with a secondary suite.  
Variance 
This variance provides approximately equivalent or greater safety performance with respect to persons and 
property as provided for by the Safety Codes Act and the NBC(AE).
